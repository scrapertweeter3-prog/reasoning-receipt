The hidden reasoning tokens are still on the bill

When a vendor summarizes its chain of thought, the receipt changes shape before the price changes. Take a support-desk deployment billed at output rates, $15 per million tokens. One call returns a 500-token answer you can read, and underneath it burns 4,000 reasoning tokens you never see. The bill covers 4,500 tokens at $0.0675 per call. Price the call off the visible 500 and you land at $0.0075, off by a factor of nine before the summary enters the picture.

The summary is where the audit trap sits. Say the vendor surfaces 100 tokens of reasoning highlights. Coverage of the hidden 4,000 is 2.5 percent. An analyst reading that receipt sees 600 tokens of model output, computes $0.009 per call, and signs off. The missing $0.0585 per call is real spend with no line item.

Volume turns the rounding error into a budget line. At 2,000 calls a day the gap is $117 daily, about $42,700 a year, billed on top of a receipt that reads $18 a day. Unit economics computed from the visible receipt run 7.5 times low, which is precisely the class of error a serving audit exists to catch.

The fix costs nothing. Ignore the prose and read the usage block: billed-token counts in the API response are the audit surface, the highlights are packaging.
